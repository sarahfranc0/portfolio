Marketing Perspective

Campaigns
I Love

Three brands doing something right, and what I think makes them work.

Depop x Coachtopia Circular Fashion Collab

Sustainability With Gen Z Appeal

The Depop x Coachtopia partnership brings Coach's circular sub-brand, built on deadstock leather and upcycled materials, directly onto the resale platform where Gen Z already shops. It frames sustainability less as a corporate checkbox and more as a genuine sense of responsibility, giving young shoppers a way to buy into the story along with the product.

What really makes it land is the choice to partner with young influencers across social media rather than relying on traditional ad placements, letting the campaign feel native to the platforms Gen Z is already spending time on.

My only critique is availability: the bags have sold out so quickly that a lot of interested buyers miss out entirely. I wish they'd keep more consistent stock, and I'd love to see the brand go a step further by actually interacting with real customers and sellers on Depop instead of just using it as a distribution channel.
Rivian Real Rivian Adventures

Authentic Stories Over Performance Theater

The Real Rivian Adventures campaign focuses on authentic, everyday stories rather than traditional performance-driven car advertising. Inspired by real owner experiences, it highlights how Rivian vehicles naturally fit into family life and spontaneous moments.

The "Last Lemonade Standing" spot is a great example, turning a playful neighborhood lemonade stand rivalry into a cinematic yet grounded story, showing how the R1T's built-in features support creativity and connection.

I really like the emphasis on family adventures because it feels genuine and relatable without being overproduced or overly sentimental. It positions the brand as adventurous but still warm and human, which makes the message land in a more meaningful way.
Patagonia Anti-Consumerism as Brand Identity

Don't Buy This Jacket

Patagonia has built a strong anti-consumerism stance directly into its brand identity, most famously through its "Don't Buy This Jacket" campaign, which challenged customers to think before purchasing. Rather than pushing constant consumption, the brand emphasizes durability, repair, and reuse through initiatives like Worn Wear, encouraging people to value experiences outdoors over accumulating more products.

What I love is that Patagonia promotes a lifestyle centered on adventure and environmental responsibility while staying true to its climate commitments. That consistency, where messaging, operations, and values all align, feels authentic rather than performative. That's rare, and it's what makes it a truly strong brand.
